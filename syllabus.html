Participation

Participation is very important in this class. It means being present for lectures, engaging with the lecture and the in-class questions, and taking part in discussions.

If you can’t make it to a lecture, please fill out the missed lecture form. You have 3 excused absences that won’t affect your participation grade. Additional absences result in a deduction unless arranged with the instructor in advance.

Late days

You have 2 individual late days and 2 group late days.

  • Individual late days can be used for any individual assignment except Assignment 0, the paper presentation, the exam, and extra credit.
  • Group late days can be used for the group milestones. Presentation dates, including the final poster session, are fixed and can’t be moved.

Each individual late day counts for you only. Each group late day counts for the entire project group: one student can’t choose to be graded on an earlier group submission to save a late day. Using a late day on one assignment doesn’t move the deadline for the next one.

All assignments and project deliverables are due by 8:00 PM on the deadline. Submissions after 8:00 PM count as one full late day, even if only a few hours late. That lets you take a full late day when you need one without agonizing over every hour.

Generative AI

You may not use AI on the assignments where you give feedback to your classmates, except to refine the language of your own original writing.

If you use generative AI to help find related work for your project paper, verify that the cited papers exist and that they actually say what the AI claims.

Academic integrity

The Honor Code is in effect for this class, and I will report any suspected violations. This section of the Engineering Honor Code pamphlet is particularly relevant:

“It is a violation of the Honor Code for students to submit, as their own, work that is not the result of their own labor and thoughts. Work that includes material derived in any way from the efforts of another author, either by direct quotation or paraphrasing, should be fully and properly documented. To avoid plagiarism, it is necessary to cite all sources of both ideas and direct quotations, including those found on the internet. The basic principle is to provide enough information so that the original source of the material can be located.”

Please see the Engineering Honor Code pamphlet for details.
