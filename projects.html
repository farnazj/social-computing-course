Past Projects

A few of the systems teams designed, launched, and evaluated in past semesters. Enrolled students can see the full posters from past semesters on Canvas.

Winter 2026

  • CopeClippy

    Aims to help people manage strong emotional reactions to upsetting posts on social media, so they respond more thoughtfully. A Chrome extension for Reddit estimates how emotionally triggering a post is. When someone starts to comment on one, it asks them to name what they feel and reflect on why before they post.

  • Social Mirror

    Aims to help people break out of doomscrolling on social media by closing the gap between what they mean to do there and what they actually do. Users set what they want to see, what to avoid, and how long to stay. A Chrome extension tracks what they actually see on Twitter, nudges them when the session drifts, and shows them the difference afterward.

  • Lyra

    Aims to give people a more active role in discovering music, which streaming algorithms now largely decide for them. Instead of ranking songs with an algorithm, Lyra lets people discover music through each other. Listeners share songs and react to them with emoji, and a shared map shows how the community's tastes relate.

Fall 2025

  • EchoBreaker

    Aims to reduce political polarization by correcting a common misperception, that the extreme views people see online are typical of the other party. Users see real posts from X, guess how common each stance is among Democrats and Republicans, and compare their guesses with national survey data, in daily rounds over a week.

  • ClearFrame

    Aims to bring local perspectives to international news, which usually reaches readers through a few distant outlets. A browser extension finds coverage of the same event from outlets near where it happened, translates it, and shows where local reporting confirms, adds to, or contradicts the article the reader is on.

  • Cooper-Bot

    Aims to bring large, quiet online communities to life, like Slack servers where hundreds of members rarely talk because posting publicly feels risky. A Slack bot privately asks members a question, groups them by the interests their answers reveal, and starts small group chats where they have a natural reason to talk.
